Buoyancy Float — Second Generation

SolidWorksFusion 360ESP32PCB design
Role
Person-in-Charge
Team
MantaRay PolyU
Timeline
Dec 2025 – present
Status
In development

Problem

  • Reaching any critical component meant removing five to eight screws, so routine maintenance was slow.
  • Recharging required pulling the battery pack, which broke the waterproof seal on every cycle and put the enclosure's integrity at risk.

What changed

  • Sealed charging. Redesigned the PCB to route charge current from an external port to the onboard cells. The hull now stays closed between runs.
  • Repacked the battery. Replaced the 8S2P arrangement with ten cells in series, holding the actuator at the 12 V it needs while keeping current headroom.
  • Access. Reorganised the internal layout so serviceable components sit behind a single panel.

Next

In development for the 2027 MATE ROV season, targeting full points on the float task.

Buoyancy Float — First Build

SolidWorksFusion 360ESP32ArduinoPCB design
Role
Person-in-Charge
Team
MantaRay PolyU
Timeline
Sep – Dec 2025
Result
3rd — MATE ROV 2026 HK Regionals

What it does

A motorised ballast system that changes its internal volume to control depth. Holding position by displacement rather than by running thrusters removes the constant power draw of station-keeping, which is what sets the ROV's endurance on a long dive.

Build

  • Mechanical. Watertight enclosure with custom O-ring grooves and a twist-action power mechanism, modelled in SolidWorks and Fusion 360 to hold its seal under hydrostatic pressure at depth.
  • Electronics. Custom PCB distributing power and routing an ESP32 to the pressure sensor and motor driver, replacing a loose wiring harness and cutting the electronics footprint.
  • Control. Arduino firmware. A bang-bang loop on pressure-derived depth drives the ballast motor to hold a setpoint against currents.

Outcome

Ran through every competition heat without a flood or a reset. Third place at the MATE ROV 2026 Hong Kong Regionals. The same approach applies to long-duration survey work, where power spent on depth-keeping is power not spent on the mission.

Context

FIRST Global runs one robotics competition a year, with a single national team from each participating country and a common kit of parts. The 2024 edition was held in Athens on the theme of food security. Team Mozambique built and tested in Maputo with Stemmoz, then competed in the tournament's alliance format alongside other national teams.

My role

  • Mechanical captain. Ran structural optimisation and CAD iterations through the build season, keeping the robot inside competition specification.
  • Primary driver. Operated the robot from the driver station across tournament matches, playing in alliances drawn against other national teams.
  • Pit crew. Daily diagnostic checks and between-match repairs to keep the robot available for every scheduled round.

The robot

Built on the FIRST Global kit: REV Control Hub and Expansion Hub, HD Hex motors, a chain-driven vertical lift, and a compliant-wheel intake sized for the field's game elements.
